**Status: Temporary orders; cases ongoing.** Neither case has a final ruling. The refugee order is a short administrative stay, and the California dispute is still before a district judge.

## The refugee rule

On Oct. 27, U.S. District Judge Julia Kobick of Massachusetts issued a 10-day administrative stay, through Nov. 6, of a USCIS rule issued Oct. 14 (The Hill; court order).

- **The rule:** it cut the time refugees have to answer termination notices from 30 days to 10.
- **The stay:** it gives the assigned judge time to consider a longer block.
- **Notices already issued:** the order also bars ending anyone's refugee status for failing to answer a short-deadline notice issued since Oct. 14.
- **Scale:** DHS estimated the rule could affect up to 186,000 refugees.
- **Who sued:** refugee advocacy groups and four refugees.

**The administration's side:** the administration defends the rule as a national-security and anti-fraud measure. USCIS called the lawsuit a "frivolous attempt to disrupt" immigration enforcement (Investing.com).

## The California ballots

The administration is seeking to keep about 20,000 mail ballots from being counted. It says they went to "likely" noncitizens (Al Jazeera; LA Times).

**The appeals court:** the 9th Circuit ruled 2-1 against sequestering the ballots.

- **The majority:** Judge Gabriel Sanchez wrote that granting the request would invite "disorder and confusion for voters and election officials alike."
- **The states' role:** the panel noted that running elections is the state's job.

**The district court:** U.S. District Judge David O. Carter (LA Times; ABC News):

- questioned how the suspect-voter list was put together
- warned about disenfranchising eligible voters
- told federal officials to stop threatening election workers
- plans to appoint a special master, which could push a final decision past Nov. 3

Carter is expected to rule Wednesday, Oct. 28.

**The administration's side:** it says the request is aimed at keeping ballots sent to likely noncitizens from being counted.
