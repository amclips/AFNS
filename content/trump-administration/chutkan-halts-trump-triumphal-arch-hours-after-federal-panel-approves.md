**Status: Confirmed by the Washington Post and CBS News.** The Hill reported the National Capital Planning Commission meeting disruption. The ruling was issued **late Thursday, Nov. 5**, and was widely reported **Friday morning, Nov. 6**.

U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan paused construction of President Trump's planned **250-foot triumphal arch** in Washington for **two weeks**, about an hour after the National Capital Planning Commission approved it, the [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/05/trump-wants-his-arch-approved-thursday-and-immediately-start-work/) reports. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-arch-approval-national-capital-planning-commission/) says the temporary restraining order bars construction-related activity — including excavation, demolition, land disturbance, utility work and roadway closures — while further proceedings play out. Chutkan wrote that plaintiffs are likely to succeed in showing the government did not follow required legal processes, CBS reports.

## Plaintiffs

The Washington Post says the challengers include **three Vietnam War veterans**, a **historic preservationist**, the **District of Columbia** government and the **Commonwealth of Virginia**.

## NCPC meeting crashed

[The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6199942-live-updates-trump-arch-midterm-elections-russia-ukraine/) reports that the commission's virtual meeting **crashed after about 90 minutes** during public testimony that was largely against the project, shortly after a tense exchange with Rep. Jared Huffman (D-Calif.). CBS says the panel later voted **8-4** to approve the plans after hours of public comment.

**No construction has been cleared to begin** under the temporary order. The court has not issued a final ruling on the project's legality.
