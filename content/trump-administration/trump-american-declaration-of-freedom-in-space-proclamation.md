**Status: White House proclamation; Axios analysis.**

On election night, Nov. 3, President Trump signed [The American Declaration of Freedom in Space](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/the-american-declaration-of-freedom-in-space/). The proclamation says "the solar system and all of outer space beyond Earth are the province of all humans" and are "open to exploration, development, use, and the establishment of a permanent presence by American citizens consistent with United States law and the international obligations of the United States."

It reaffirms goals of returning to the Moon by 2028, laying a foundation for a permanent American lunar presence and eventually landing an American on Mars.

## Largely symbolic, Axios says

[Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/04/trump-space-proclamation-election-night) describes the edict as mostly symbolic. It says the language appears to stay in line with the 1967 Outer Space Treaty's ban on national appropriation and with the Artemis Accords. Axios notes China's reported goal of a lunar landing before 2030. Victoria Samson of the Secure World Foundation told Axios the 2028 lunar return target is "wildly optimistic." The White House did not immediately respond to Axios.
