**Updated Oct. 28, 2026, 9 AM CT.** This article now uses the Central Elections Committee's partial results at 85.6% counted. They replace the earlier partial figures from Wednesday morning and the election-night early count. First published Oct. 27.

**Status: Partial results; no winner declared.** Counting is paused for the day and is not finished, so **seats can still move**. No camp has a majority.

## Where the count stands

With 85.6% of votes counted, the Central Elections Committee's partial results, as reported by the Times of Israel, are:

- **Netanyahu bloc:** 53 seats, eight short of the 61 needed for a majority
- **Eisenkot bloc:** 48 seats
- **Arab parties:** 19 seats, a record
- **Largest parties:** Likud 26, Yashar 21

That is slightly worse for Netanyahu's bloc than Wednesday morning's partial count.

**The trend:** overnight, at about 42% counted, Netanyahu's bloc was as high as 57 seats. It has slipped since, while Eisenkot's side and the Arab parties have gained.

## What's left to count, and when

- **Paused for the day:** no more updates are expected Wednesday (Times of Israel).
- **Extra checks:** about 7% of regular ballots need additional checks.
- **Double-envelope ballots:** about 600,000 are to be added over the next couple of days. They include ballots cast by soldiers, diplomats, hospital patients and prisoners.
- **Official results:** President Isaac Herzog expects them next week. CNBC reported final results are expected around Nov. 4.
- **Coalition talks:** AP says weeks of talks could lie ahead.

## Herzog urges parties to avoid a repeat election

In his first comments since the vote, Herzog urged the parties to avoid another election. He asked them to come to coalition talks "with an open mind." He said he expects official results next week, with his consultations on who forms the government to follow (Times of Israel).

## Turnout

Turnout was 77.7%, the highest in more than 25 years.

## The Arab parties are the swing question

Without Arab party support, the anti-Netanyahu bloc can't reach 61 on the current numbers. Neither side can form a government without breaking a campaign promise or bringing in an unlikely partner.

- **Netanyahu** called it "forbidden" to form a government that relies on Arab parties (Times of Israel).
- **Eisenkot** has said he sees no Arab party that accepts his conditions. They include recognizing Hamas as a terrorist group that must be dismantled (Times of Israel; i24NEWS analysis).
- **Joint List chair Yousef Jabareen** said anyone who wants to replace Netanyahu "will have to talk to us." He said his party is "ready to pave the way" to oust Netanyahu (Times of Israel).
- **Yair Golan** is urging a coalition with the Arab parties (Times of Israel).

**UNCONFIRMED (based on an X trending summary; not independently confirmed):** according to the summary, Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas would not call Hamas terrorists in a live interview. It says he accepted the first three of Eisenkot's coalition conditions but refused the fourth, labeling Hamas a terrorist group, while saying he condemns October 7. AFNS has not reviewed the interview.

## The opposition moves on the Knesset speakership

According to Channel 12, as reported by the Times of Israel, the Eisenkot bloc wants to use its votes plus the Arab parties' votes to replace Likud's Amir Ohana as Knesset speaker. Oded Forer and Elazar Stern are among the names discussed.

## Markets react

The shekel weakened and the Tel Aviv stock exchange slumped (Times of Israel).

## Both leaders claim the mandate

**Netanyahu** told supporters that Likud is the biggest party and that the right has a "decisive majority among the Jewish public." He vowed to remove the Iranian threat "together with our wonderful friend President Trump" (Times of Israel).

**Eisenkot** said Netanyahu faces a "larger and stronger Zionist majority" and should leave political life. He also said another election would be "a mistake" (Times of Israel).

AFP reported that both leaders vowed to form the next government.

## Trump weighs in

- **On Truth Social,** President Trump shared a quote saying "the whole tide turned" after he endorsed Netanyahu (Times of Israel).
- **To reporters,** Trump said the endorsement "helped him a lot" and that the result looks like "a stalemate or a win." He said he has not spoken to Netanyahu yet (Times of Israel; Jerusalem Post).
- **Unconfirmed, based on anonymous sources:** ynet cites unnamed sources close to Trump who say he does not want Ben-Gvir, and probably not Smotrich, in the next government. AFNS has not confirmed this, and the White House has not said it publicly.

## Other reactions from election night

- **Lapid** called the result a "total defeat" for Netanyahu (via Amichai Stein).
- **Bennett** said the opposition is coordinating and that Netanyahu's "coalition of draft-evaders has collapsed" (via i24NEWS).
- **Yair Golan** said voters "fired Netanyahu and the Likud" (via the Jerusalem Post).
- **Mansour Abbas** of Ra'am said he hopes to help the opposition reach 61 (via Israel News Pulse).
- **The Likud camp** did not concede. Sa'ar called opposition celebrations "premature" (via Kan News).

## The exit polls, for the record

On election night, the final exit polls were split (Reuters). Kan and Channel 13 had the camps tied at 51–51, and Channel 12 had Netanyahu's bloc ahead 52–49. Exit polls are projections, and the official count above supersedes them.
