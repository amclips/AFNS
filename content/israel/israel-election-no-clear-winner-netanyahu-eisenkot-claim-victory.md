**Status: Partial results; no winner declared.** With about 31% of votes counted, these numbers are early and **will shift** as counting continues. No camp has been declared the winner. Figures are as of 11 PM CT on Oct. 27.

## Where the count stands

The partial official count, at about 31% of votes counted, per ynet:

- Netanyahu bloc: 59 seats
- Eisenkot-led opposition: 46 seats
- Arab parties: 15 seats
- Largest parties: Likud 29, Yashar 20

A majority in the 120-seat Knesset takes 61 seats. Neither side clearly has it, CNBC reported. Netanyahu's bloc won 64 seats in 2022.

## The exit polls

**Final exit polls** (Reuters): Kan and Channel 13 both have the two camps tied at 51–51. Channel 12 has Netanyahu's bloc ahead 52–49. All three give Likud 27 seats.

**Earlier exit polls** (3:20 PM CT), as reported by the Times of Israel, AP and Axios:

- **Channel 12:** opposition 52, Netanyahu bloc 50, Arab parties 18. Likud 24, Yashar 22, B'Yachad 12, Joint List 11.
- **Channel 13:** opposition 53, Netanyahu bloc 50, Arab parties 17. Likud 25, Yashar 23.
- **Kan:** opposition 53, Netanyahu bloc 50, Arab parties 17. Likud 25, Yashar 21, B'Yachad 14, Joint List 10, Democrats 10, Yisrael Beytenu 8, Otzma Yehudit 7, Ra'am 7, Shas 6, UTJ 6, Religious Zionism 6.

Channel 14 gave Netanyahu's bloc 60 seats and i24NEWS gave it 55. Hendel's Reservists and Ofer Winter's People of Israel were projected to fall below the 3.25% threshold needed to enter the Knesset.

The early and final polls differ, and both differ from the partial count. Only the official count decides the result.

## Turnout

Final turnout was 77.7%, the highest since 1999, according to The Guardian. It also reported record Arab turnout, citing 70.1% as the figure at the same point in 2022.

## What happens next

- **By Thursday:** soldiers' ballots fully counted (CNBC).
- **Friday:** the special-ballot count. Liberman says to wait for it before drawing conclusions (CNBC).
- **By Nov. 4:** official results published (CNBC).

## What the leaders said

**Netanyahu** claimed victory in a speech, according to AP, Barak Ravid and Ynetnews. He said the right has a "decisive majority" and called for a "strong, right-wing" government. He did not mention a unity government with Eisenkot. He said Iran is weaker but "it is not over," and that Israel must eliminate the threat together with President Trump.

**Eisenkot** also declared victory, according to Tal Schneider, i24NEWS and Amichai Stein. He said the result shows "complete no-confidence in Netanyahu and the October 7 government." He wants the first chance to form a government, said a second election "would be a mistake," and promised a "Zionist draft government." He warned Netanyahu not to use a possible Iran strike for political gain.

AFP reported that both leaders vowed to form the next government.

## Other reactions

- **Lapid** called the result a "total defeat" for Netanyahu (via Amichai Stein).
- **Bennett** said the opposition is coordinating and that Netanyahu's "coalition of draft-evaders has collapsed" (via i24NEWS).
- **Yair Golan** said voters "fired Netanyahu and the Likud" (via the Jerusalem Post).
- **Mansour Abbas** of Ra'am said he hopes to help the opposition reach 61. He called Arab turnout "despair and frustration that turned into hope" and said the right "suffered a major loss" (via Israel News Pulse). Most opposition leaders except Golan have ruled out relying on Arab parties.
- **The Likud camp is not conceding.** Sa'ar called the celebrations "premature," and supporters at Likud headquarters chanted "Only Bibi!" (via Kan News).
- **Betting markets:** earlier in the day, Kalshi gave Eisenkot about a 58% chance of becoming prime minister.

## Why it matters for Americans

The next Israeli government will shape the Iran conflict, where the United States is directly involved. Netanyahu said Israel must finish the Iran threat together with President Trump, who endorsed him. Eisenkot warned against using a strike for political gain.
