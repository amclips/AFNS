## Iran and the United States

- **Post-midterms campaign.** The U.S. told Gulf states to prepare for a "short, intense military campaign" after next week's U.S. midterms on Nov. 3, the Wall Street Journal reported.
- **The cost so far.** U.S. spending on the Iran war is above $47 billion, more than 25% over the Pentagon's July estimate, Reuters reported.
- **Talks in Tehran.** Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks in Tehran to head off a new war. There was no concrete outcome, CBS News reported.
- **Toronto shootings (alleged).** Iran allegedly paid for about 12 shootings in Toronto, at synagogues, a school and the U.S. consulate, CBC reported, according to an X trending summary. No one was injured. These are allegations.
- **Travel advice.** The UK updated its travel advice for 15 Middle East countries, including Israel and Iran.

**Why it matters for Americans:** Reuters' figure is money U.S. taxpayers have already spent, and it is running more than a quarter above the Pentagon's own estimate. The WSJ report means more spending could come right after Americans vote on Nov. 3. The alleged Toronto plot included a U.S. diplomatic post.

## West Bank

- **Hebron.** A crackdown in Hebron after two soldiers were killed has displaced more than 3,000 Palestinians, UN News reported. The UN's Türk called it collective punishment.
- **UK MPs turned back.** British MPs were held for eight hours and denied entry to the West Bank at the Allenby crossing.

## Gaza

- **Strikes.** Strikes reportedly killed eight people, including a child, according to Conflict Tracker. AFNS has not independently confirmed the figure.
- **Ben-Gvir.** Ben-Gvir called for conquering all of Gaza and encouraging emigration.

## Lebanon

- **Hezbollah.** Hezbollah is rebuilding with Iranian money, while Israel carries out strikes and ground pushes in south Lebanon, L'Orient Today reported.

## Other

- **Eurovision.** The Netherlands withdrew from Eurovision 2027, citing Israel's participation.
- **Bank of Israel.** The Bank of Israel rejected Netanyahu's idea of a crypto "bypass economy," ABC News (Australia) reported.
