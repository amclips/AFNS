## Update, Nov. 5, 9 AM CT

The Joint List, an alliance of Arab-led parties with 11 Knesset seats, announced it will recommend Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/ea4mo8z60), [Ynet follow-up](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/rydawnytgl)). Ynet says that brings Eisenkot to **67 recommendations** against **46** for Benjamin Netanyahu — past the 61-seat majority mark (56 + 11 = 67). [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/israel-s-eisenkot-set-to-secure-67-recommendations-after-arab-joint-list-announces-support/4113117) reports the same figures, citing Ynet. The Joint List said the decision was unanimous and that recommending a prime ministerial candidate was "not a simple decision"; the alliance has rarely recommended any PM candidate.

**Herzog has not assigned the mandate.** No governing coalition has been agreed. Recommendations are not a coalition. Posts saying Herzog could task Eisenkot as soon as this evening remain **unconfirmed**.


## Update, Nov. 5

On day two of President Herzog's consultations, Ra'am, Yair Golan's Democrats and Yisrael Beytenu recommended Gadi Eisenkot. United Torah Judaism — Netanyahu's partner after the last five elections — recommended nobody ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/united-torah-judaism-declines-to-endorse-candidate-for-prime-minister-in-herzog-consultations/), [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-913890), [RTE](https://www.rte.ie/news/middle-east/2026/1105/1598104-israel-government/)). The Times of Israel now tallies **56 recommendations for Eisenkot and 46 for Netanyahu**. That supersedes yesterday's mid-consultation 56-to-53 ToI count: UTJ's seven seats left Netanyahu's recommendation total without them. Likud, Yashar, B'Yachad and the Joint List meet Herzog later Thursday. Herzog usually gives the mandate to whoever has the most recommendations, so Eisenkot is expected to get it; the designee has 28 days plus a possible 14-day extension, and failure could mean another election early next year.


## Update, Nov. 4, 3 PM CT

The Arab-led Ra'am party said it would recommend Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/dtwgv35tf), [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/raam-will-recommend-eisenkot-for-pm-likely-giving-him-first-shot-at-forming-government/)). The Times of Israel's mid-consultation tally that afternoon was 56 recommendations for Eisenkot and 53 for Netanyahu, with the Joint List's 11 seats possibly abstaining. Those were outlet tallies, not Herzog's official count. Neither candidate has a 61-seat majority, and the Times of Israel notes Eisenkot pledged before the election not to include Ra'am in his government. (An X post that day also floated 56 to 46; that figure matched ToI's later day-two tally after UTJ abstained — see the Nov. 5 update below.)

In his first video statement since the election, Netanyahu warned that "the one government that certainly will not be able to confront the threat of radical Islam is a government that depends on radical Islam," and accused opposition leaders of working toward "a narrow minority government" ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1x0000wfafe), [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/435681)). He called for a broad national government led by Likud.

**Status: Results certified; consultations under way.**

## Results certified

President Isaac Herzog accepted the certified results of the Oct. 27 election. The Central Elections Committee says it audited 9,069 polling stations and found no fraud that changed the outcome. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/israel-election-netanyahu-57aefc6424da937ad2842f6d762631ae) and [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/4/israel-vote-results-confirmed-as-election-commission-rejects-fraud-claims) report that the finding undercuts Netanyahu's claims that the election was stolen.

The final seat count is 53 for Netanyahu's bloc, 48 for Eisenkot's bloc and a record 19 for the Arab parties.

## Herzog's remarks

According to the [Times of Israel's full text](https://www.timesofisrael.com/full-text-of-president-herzogs-remarks-on-receiving-official-2026-election-results/) of his remarks, Herzog urged compromise, condemned "racist" rhetoric against Arab citizens and warned against going to another election. He also noted that today marks 31 years since the assassination of Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin.

## Consultations today and Thursday

Herzog's consultations with the parties run today and Thursday. [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/syxx2dutfg) expects 48 Knesset members to recommend Eisenkot and 46 to recommend Netanyahu. The Joint List, Ra'am and United Torah Judaism are undecided. Whoever Herzog picks will have 28 days to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension.

Eisenkot, Bennett, Lapid, Golan and Liberman have agreed to recommend Eisenkot. Ynet reports Eisenkot is leaning toward a minority government and courting the Haredi parties ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1n0nndtme)). Separately, Shas and two far-right parties are backing Netanyahu, according to reports in our [world briefing](/geopolitics/world-briefing-2026-11-04/).

**Update, Nov. 4, 1 PM CT:** The prediction market Kalshi gives Eisenkot a 54% chance of becoming Israel's next prime minister ([Kalshi on X](https://x.com/KalshiPolitics/status/2118042814848057370)). That is a market price set by traders, not a poll.

## Clash over the Shin Bet election probe

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara rejected a request from Shin Bet chief David Zini to question Arab MKs Mansour Abbas and Ahmad Tibi, saying there was no evidence. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir had urged arrests anyway. The [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/2026-11-04/live-updates-913771) says Zini now appears to be backing off ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/syxx2dutfg)).
