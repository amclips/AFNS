**Status: Charged; presumed innocent.** These are charges, not convictions. Collins's attorney says he didn't break the law. Both council members are presumed innocent.

## The charges

Muskegon County Prosecutor DJ Hilson charged two Muskegon Heights City Council members (MLive):

- **Derrick Lamar Collins, 61:** a felony count of public servant contracting to purchase residential property. It concerns 2301 Reynolds Ave.
- **Lashae Felice Simmons, 62:** a misdemeanor count of willful neglect of duty by a public official. It concerns a land deal at 3327 Eighth St.

## The prosecutor's memo

Hilson's Oct. 27 memo says the council voted in 2021 to sell the Reynolds property to Collins, and the city attorney prepared the deed in 2025.

- **The law:** Michigan law generally bars public servants from contracting with the bodies they serve. The memo says the residential exception doesn't apply to elected officials.
- **Simmons:** the memo doesn't spell out her alleged conduct. Hilson said the details will come out in court.
- **A prior warning:** in January 2025, an interim city manager's memo warned about conflicts on tax-reverted properties. Then-Mayor Bonnie McGlothin forwarded it to council members, including both Collins and Simmons.

## The wider investigation

The case grew out of the July 23 search of City Hall. The prosecutor called it "an active financial and drug crime investigation."

- **Agencies:** the FBI, DEA, IRS and Michigan State Police are involved.
- **Scale:** more than 7,000 digital items have been reviewed.
- **The former city manager:** Shawnderrick Roberson faces separate drug and state tax-evasion charges.

"Today's charges and the release of the investigative memorandum are not the close of this investigation," Hilson said.

## The defense

Collins's attorney says Collins didn't break the law. Simmons's response was not in the source.
