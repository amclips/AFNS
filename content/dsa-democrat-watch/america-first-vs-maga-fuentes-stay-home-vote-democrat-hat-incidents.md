**Status: Claims unverified.** Fuentes's statements are documented by the Washington Times and Newsweek. The hat incidents below are **UNVERIFIED**.

**A note on the name:** in this article, "America First" refers to Nick Fuentes's movement and its branding, including its hats. AFNS uses "America First" in its own tagline, but it has no affiliation with Fuentes or his movement.

## Who Fuentes is

The Washington Times calls Fuentes an extremist. Newsweek calls him a far-right Gen Z influencer.

## "Stay home or vote Democrat"

On Oct. 26, Fuentes said President Trump broke his "no new wars" promise over the Iran war and the economy. He told followers to "stay home or vote Democrat" (Washington Times; Newsweek). For his post and its reach, see [yesterday's report](../../trump-administration/fuentes-stay-home-or-vote-democrat-post/).

## Ohio: Acton vs. Ramaswamy

- **The endorsement:** Fuentes urged Ohioans to back Democrat Amy Acton over Republican Vivek Ramaswamy for governor. The Cincinnati Exchange traced this to his Oct. 8-9 posts.
- **Acton:** there is no sign Acton has accepted the endorsement.
- **Trump:** the President campaigns in Ohio for Ramaswamy today.

## The hat incidents (UNVERIFIED)

- **UNVERIFIED: the Vance clip.** A clip with about 2.2 million views appears to show a young man in an America First hat being escorted out from behind JD Vance (@ImperiumFirst). No outlet has confirmed the venue or the reason. Derrick Evans and pollster Mark Mitchell criticized Vance over it on X.
- **UNVERIFIED: Fort Myers.** X posts claim Trump rally staff in Fort Myers made people remove America First hats or leave (@BarronTruthX).
- **UNVERIFIED: the hat call.** Fan accounts say Fuentes urged supporters to wear the hats to MAGA rallies.

## Why it matters

Fuentes is urging part of the right to sit out or vote for Democrats a week before the midterms, in races such as Ohio's. How much of the vote he can move is not documented in the sources.
