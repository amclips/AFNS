**Status: Procedural order confirmed.** This is not a decision on whether 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) is constitutional.

In [Temple Gun Club v. Blanche](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/26-10696/26-10696-2026-11-04.html), No. 26-10696, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit on Wednesday denied a petition for initial hearing en banc ([court PDF](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/26/26-10696-CV0.pdf)). After a poll of active judges, 8 voted in favor and 9 against. The per curiam order means the Commerce Clause challenge to the federal ban on transferring and possessing machine guns (§ 922(o)) will be heard by an ordinary three-judge panel, not the full court first.

Judges voting for initial en banc hearing were Chief Judge Elrod and Judges Smith, Higginson, Willett, Ho, Duncan, Oldham and Douglas. Voting against were Judges Jones, Stewart, Richman, Southwick, Haynes, Graves, Engelhardt, Wilson and Ramirez.

## The dissent

Judge Andrew Oldham, joined by Judge James Ho, dissented from the denial. Quoting his earlier writing, he said, "As far as I am aware, there is no historical justification for banning machine guns," and argued the court should not limit its Commerce Clause reconsideration to § 922(g) in United States v. Squire. He wrote that § 922(o) lacks an interstate-commerce jurisdictional hook and is "arguably more" vulnerable under Lopez and Morrison. The denial does not decide those questions.
