**Status: West Virginia Supreme Court opinion in State v. Crawford, No. 24-102** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/west-virginia/supreme-court/2026/24-102.html); [WV News](https://www.wvnews.com/news/wvnews/state-supreme-court-curbs-surrogate-expert-testimony-narrows-gun-statute-in-drug-ruling/article_3b9b116f-c1af-43f2-8676-e8f61c361c21.html)).

## Surrogate lab testimony barred

The court held that the Confrontation Clause of the Sixth Amendment and Article III, section 14 of the West Virginia Constitution bar introducing a laboratory report through an expert who **neither performed nor observed** the underlying test. Reviewer Blake Kinder introduced analyst Tiffany Neu's findings after she left the state police lab. Kinder had signed as reviewer but did not independently test the evidence; agreeing with Neu's conclusions did not make them his own opinion, the court said.

Because the drug identifications depended on Neu's report, the court ordered a **new trial** on two possession-with-intent-to-deliver counts. That was trial error, so **double jeopardy does not bar retrial** of those counts.

## Felony firearm use narrowed

Separately, the court held that "use" of a firearm in connection with a felony under West Virginia Code **61-7-15a** requires **active employment** — brandishing, displaying, firing, striking, or verbally referencing the weapon to change the circumstances of the felony — not mere passive possession or storage. A loaded gun stored with drugs in a fanny pack beneath a car seat did not qualify. That conviction became an **acquittal that cannot be retried**.

Jacquain Crawford's convictions for prohibited-person firearm possession and carrying a concealed firearm **remain**.
