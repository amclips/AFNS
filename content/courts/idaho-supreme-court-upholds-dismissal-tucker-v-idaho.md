**Status: Dismissal upheld 3-2.** The court left room for a future lawsuit.

## What happened

On Oct. 28, the Idaho Supreme Court voted 3-2 to uphold the dismissal of Tucker v. Idaho ([opinion](https://api.isc.idaho.gov/uploads/Documents/ISC/Civil/2026/1793203825047-51631.pdf)). The ACLU filed the Sixth Amendment suit in 2015, when each of Idaho's 44 counties ran its own public defense, and argued caseloads were too high. An Ada County judge dismissed it in February 2024, after the Legislature created a statewide system that took over in October 2024 ([Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/28/idaho-supreme-court-upholds-dismissal-of-legal-cha/)).

## The majority

Chief Justice Robyn Brody, joined by Justices Moeller and Bevan, wrote that this "is not a case in which a private actor… simply promises to do better," but one where elected officials "comprehensively restructured a public institution." Whether the new law will "ultimately succeed" is "not for us to decide today," she wrote.

## The dissent

Justices Meyer and Burdick dissented. They said the case was not moot and that the new system has not been shown to fix the problems. Meyer wrote that the majority relied on a mootness framework Idaho law never adopted and the Ninth Circuit does not use.

## Reaction

The ACLU of Idaho called the ruling a disappointment and said it is weighing its options ([ACLU of Idaho](https://www.acluidaho.org/press-releases/idaho-supreme-court-affirms-lower-court-ruling-to-dismiss-public-defense-case/)). The ACLU says hundreds, if not thousands, of defendants went without lawyers during the new office's rocky start. That is the ACLU's claim.
