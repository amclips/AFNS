**Status: Revived; claims not yet decided.** The appeals court sent the case back for more proceedings. It did not rule that McNellis was discriminated against. His claims remain allegations. The decision is an **unpublished order and judgment, so it is not binding precedent.**

## The ruling

On Tuesday, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit revived a religious discrimination lawsuit by Corey McNellis. He is a former athletic director and assistant principal at Ponderosa High School in Douglas County, Colorado (10th Cir. order).

- **The panel:** Judges Eid and Carson, with District Judge Wyrick sitting by designation.
- **The decision:** the panel reversed a grant of summary judgment for the Douglas County School District and sent the case back for further proceedings.
- **The reason:** it found a genuine dispute of material fact on whether the district's stated reasons for the firing were pretext.

## What McNellis alleges

McNellis voiced reservations in a staff email chain about an upcoming extracurricular production of "The Laramie Project." He offered to add a "Christian perspective." He was then placed on administrative leave, investigated and fired.

"The Laramie Project" is a play about the murder of a gay college student (Bloomberg Law).

He sued in July 2022. He brought a First Amendment retaliation claim, plus religious discrimination and retaliation claims under Title VII and Colorado law.

## The evidence the panel cited

- **The principal's letter:** a letter from the school's then-principal saying McNellis was "railroaded" for his "religious views."
- **McNellis's testimony:** he testified that a district official told him he "was being put on leave because of [his] religious remarks."
- **The investigation:** the panel said that evidence, along with the district's investigation, raises the question of whether he was fired for his protected religious beliefs.

## How the case got here

- **First dismissal:** the district court first dismissed the case.
- **2024:** a prior Tenth Circuit panel reversed the dismissal of his discrimination claims.
- **December 2025:** on remand, the district court granted summary judgment to the school district. It found no direct evidence of discrimination and no genuine dispute over pretext.
- **Now:** the new panel disagreed, and the case returns to the U.S. District Court in Colorado.

The school district's response to the ruling was not in the sources.
