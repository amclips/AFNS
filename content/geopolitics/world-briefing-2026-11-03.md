## Iran: strikes and talks at the same time

U.S. officials describe limited "defensive" strikes on Iranian targets around Hormuz, including on Qeshm Island, and the WSJ reports they are part of a series of quiet strikes in recent weeks. Iran's foreign minister calls the indirect talks "very intensive," and Vice President JD Vance says there has been "progress" ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-news-november-2-2026-8722c8b5a9deb228e7a188d4760ea8ed), [Nhan Dan](https://en.nhandan.vn/world-news-in-brief-november-3-post168623.html)).

## Qatar mediates; Netanyahu presses Trump

Qatari mediators spent more than 12 hours in talks in Tehran, a draft proposal is circulating, and another Qatari delegation is expected. Axios reports that Netanyahu wants military action to resume right after the U.S. midterms, but that Trump is favoring diplomacy for now (as relayed by [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/162991/early-edition-november-2-2026/) and [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/morning-briefing-nov-2-2026/4108322)). Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has ordered an "immediate" response to any new attack.

## Hormuz still isn't safe for shipping

The head of the International Maritime Organization wouldn't confirm that mines have been cleared and is still advising ships not to pass through the Strait of Hormuz ([Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261103/9e33353149e54a98ad0f3f5f26782736/c.html)).

## Ukraine: strike on an aid site in Zaporizhzhia region

A Russian strike on an aid distribution site in Zaporizhzhia region killed at least 18 people. Aerial bombs hit a school in Komyshuvakha where people were collecting aid. Ukraine reports 18 dead and 27 missing, while some outlets cite 26 dead. **The toll is still changing.** The strike came right after the six-day Oleshky truce ended, and Kyiv says Russia used the pause to reinforce ([ISW](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-november-2-2026/), [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261103/9e33353149e54a98ad0f3f5f26782736/c.html)).

## Abu Dhabi talks and a Patriot sale

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff announced working-level Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10-11. The WSJ reports the U.S. wants Russian gas to flow to Europe through Ukraine again as part of an energy ceasefire, which European leaders oppose. The U.S. also approved a $2.1 billion sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 missiles to Ukraine, paid for by Canada, Germany and Norway ([Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/morning-briefing-nov-3-2026/4109288), [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/162991/early-edition-november-2-2026/)).

## Fake alerts in Ukraine and Russia

Hackers sent fake nuclear and air-raid alerts through the emergency alert apps in both Ukraine and Russia. Kyiv blames Moscow, and ISW assesses that Ukrainian hackers likely hit Russia's app in return. **UNCONFIRMED ATTRIBUTION:** neither claim has been independently confirmed. Separately, the U.S. warned Russia about a credible terror threat in Moscow ([Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/morning-briefing-nov-2-2026/4108322), [ISW](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-november-2-2026/)).

## Europe: sabotage warnings and a Belgian crisis

Suspected cyberattacks changed fridge and freezer temperatures in supermarkets in seven countries. Poland sent counterterrorism police to the Kaliningrad border after sabotage warnings. Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered to resign after budget talks collapsed, and the king hasn't decided whether to accept ([10 Things](https://www.10things.news/p/10-things-global-news-3rd-november-4b6), [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261103/9e33353149e54a98ad0f3f5f26782736/c.html)).
